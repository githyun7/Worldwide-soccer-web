FIFA WOMEN'S WORLD CUP

First Held 1991
Frequency Every 4 Years
Most Titles USA (4)
Current Champion Spain (2023)

The FIFA Women’s World Cup is the most important international tournament in women’s soccer, bringing together the top national teams from around the world. First held in 1991, the competition has grown rapidly in popularity, visibility, and quality, becoming one of the most influential sporting events on the global stage. The tournament is held every four years and showcases the highest level of women’s football.

Over the years, the Women’s World Cup has produced iconic players such as Mia Hamm, Marta, Abby Wambach, and Alexia Putellas, whose performances have inspired millions of fans and young athletes. The tournament is known for its intense competition, technical skill, and passionate fan support, demonstrating the continued rise of women’s soccer worldwide.

Beyond competition, the FIFA Women’s World Cup plays a crucial role in promoting gender equality and expanding opportunities for women in sports. It has helped accelerate investment in women’s leagues, youth development programs, and grassroots initiatives across many countries. As the tournament continues to grow, it stands as a powerful symbol of progress, inclusivity, and the global reach of soccer.
